About

Applied Labor Economics · Policy Evaluation · Family Economics · Education · Migration · Digitalization

I am a tenured Senior Researcher in the Department of Families and Family Policies at the German Youth Institute (Deutsches Jugendinstitut, DJI) in Munich.

As an applied labor economist, my aim is to conduct relevant research that enables policymakers to make evidence-based decisions. In addition to topics in migration economics, I am particularly interested in the blessings and curses of the digital transformation, which includes the impact of technology diffusion on local economies and the impact of telework and digital media in families. I have a deep passion for data and econometric methods of causal inference and policy evaluation.

Research Projects

My current research is embedded in the work of the German Youth Institute, particularly its research on families, family policy, and the AID:A survey.

  • AID:A – Growing Up in Germany: Research on the living conditions and development of children, adolescents, young adults, and their families.
  • Families and Family Policy: Empirical research on family circumstances, parental employment, care work, and family well-being.
  • Post-Separation Families: Research using the Growing Up in Germany panel to understand family circumstances following parental separation.
  • Digitalization and Work: Research on working from home, digital infrastructure, and the organization of paid and unpaid work.
